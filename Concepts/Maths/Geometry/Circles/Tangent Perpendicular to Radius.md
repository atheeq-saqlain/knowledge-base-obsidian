# Tangent Perpendicular to Radius

## Definition

The [[Tangent to a Circle]] at any point is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact.

## Description

If O is the centre and P the point of contact of tangent XY, then OP ⊥ XY. Proof idea: for any other point Q on the tangent, OQ > OP, so OP is the shortest distance from O to the line and is therefore perpendicular to it. Deduction: there is exactly one tangent at a given point of the circle. With Pythagoras, OP radius, OQ (or OP to external point) hypotenuse, and the tangent segment form a right triangle used to find lengths. A chord of a larger concentric circle that touches a smaller concentric circle is bisected at the point of contact because the common radius is perpendicular to that chord.
