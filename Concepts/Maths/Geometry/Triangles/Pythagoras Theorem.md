# Pythagoras Theorem

## Definition

In a right triangle, the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides. Conversely, if the square of one side equals the sum of the squares of the other two, the angle opposite the first side is a right angle. One standard proof uses [[Right Triangle Altitude Similarity]].

## Description

For right ΔABC with right angle at B: AC² = AB² + BC². The altitude to the hypotenuse creates two smaller right triangles similar to the original; rearranging the resulting proportions yields the theorem.

Used for ladder-and-wall problems, guy wires, distances between points or plane paths, and checking whether given side lengths form a right triangle (converse). Related identities include AB² = 2AC² for an isosceles right triangle right-angled at C.
