# Pythagoras Theorem

## Definition

In a right triangle, the square of the hypotenuse equals the sum of the squares of the other two sides. Conversely, if the square of one side equals the sum of the squares of the other two, the angle opposite the first side is a right angle.

## Description

For right ΔABC with right angle at B: AC² = AB² + BC². The proof via similarity uses the altitude to the hypotenuse: the two smaller right triangles are similar to each other and to the original triangle.

Used for ladder/wall problems, distances, and checking whether given side lengths form a right triangle.
