# Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

## Definition

Every composite number can be expressed as a product of primes, and this factorisation is unique apart from the order of the factors. In standard form, x = p₁^{a₁} p₂^{a₂} ⋯ pₙ^{aₙ} with primes in non-decreasing order.

## Description

Example: 32760 = 2³ × 3² × 5 × 7 × 13. Uniqueness implies that if a number’s prime factors never include 5, it cannot end with the digit 0. The theorem also underpins [[HCF and LCM by Prime Factorisation]] and proofs that √p is irrational for a prime p ([[Proof by Contradiction for Irrationality]]).
